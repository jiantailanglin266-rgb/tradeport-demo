Central Banks and Forex: How Interest Rates Drive Currencies

Interest rate differentials are the gravitational field of the currency market. Understand how the Fed, ECB and BOJ set policy, what forward guidance means, and why markets move on words as much as decisions.

Author: K. TanakaReviewed by: H. SuzukiPublished: Jun 20, 2026Updated: Jul 12, 2026Data last checked: Jul 12, 2026

Key Takeaways

  • ✓Capital flows toward higher real yields, strengthening those currencies over time.
  • ✓Markets price expected policy paths — surprises reprice them violently.
  • ✓Forward guidance and press conferences move markets as much as decisions.
  • ✓Carry trades earn the rate differential but carry sharp reversal risk.
  • ✓Watch real (inflation-adjusted) rates, not just nominal headlines.

Why do interest rates move currencies?

Global capital seeks yield. When one economy's interest rates rise relative to another's, holding that currency becomes more attractive, drawing inflows that bid it up. This rate differential logic underpins long-lasting FX trends.

What matters is the expected path, not the current level. A central bank holding rates but signaling hikes can strengthen its currency more than one that hikes while signaling a pause.

What should you know about the major central banks?

The Federal Reserve sets the tone for global markets; its FOMC decisions and the chair's press conference are the calendar's heavyweight events. The ECB governs the euro with its own meeting cycle, and the Bank of Japan's policy stance has historically anchored yen crosses and global carry trades.

Each bank publishes statements, minutes and projections. Traders parse wording changes — a single altered phrase in guidance can reprice an entire rate path.

How do traders use this in practice?

Carry strategies buy higher-yielding currencies against lower-yielding ones, collecting swap daily. The risk: carry unwinds are fast and brutal when risk sentiment turns, as crowded positions exit together.

For most traders the practical takeaway is situational awareness: know each pair's rate story, mark decision dates, and expect regime shifts when a bank pivots. Fighting a central bank's clearly stated direction is historically expensive.

Frequently Asked Questions

›What is forward guidance?

Communication about the likely future path of policy. By shaping expectations, guidance moves markets before any actual rate change — which is precisely its purpose.

›What is a carry trade?

Borrowing (selling) a low-rate currency to hold a high-rate one, earning the differential via swap. Profitable in calm markets; vulnerable to sharp reversals in risk-off episodes.

›Why does the currency sometimes fall after a rate hike?

The hike was already priced in, and the accompanying guidance disappointed — 'buy the rumor, sell the fact'. The expected path matters more than the single decision.
