How to Use the Economic Calendar: NFP, CPI and Rate Decisions

Scheduled economic releases move markets in seconds. Learn which indicators matter most, how 'expected vs actual' drives the reaction, and how traders manage positions around high-impact news.

Author: K. TanakaPublished: Jun 14, 2026Updated: Jul 5, 2026Data last checked: Jul 5, 2026

Key Takeaways

  • ✓Markets react to the gap between actual figures and consensus expectations.
  • ✓Highest impact: central bank rate decisions, CPI inflation and US Non-Farm Payrolls.
  • ✓Spreads widen and liquidity thins violently around releases.
  • ✓Trading the first seconds of a release is a specialized, high-risk game.
  • ✓Most traders do better managing existing positions than betting on the print.

Why do economic releases move prices?

Prices embed expectations. Before a release, consensus forecasts are already priced in — so the market's reaction is driven by the surprise: actual minus expected. A 'good' number below expectations can sell off a currency.

The revision of previous figures matters too, and sometimes more. Always read the release as a package: actual, consensus, prior and revision.

Which releases deserve your attention?

Top tier: central bank rate decisions and press conferences, CPI inflation, and US Non-Farm Payrolls. Second tier: GDP, PMIs, retail sales and employment data outside the US. Impact depends on what the central bank currently cares about — in an inflation-fighting cycle, CPI is king.

Calendar tools rate expected impact, but ratings are generic. Track how your instruments actually respond for a few weeks to calibrate what 'high impact' means for your pairs.

How should you trade around news?

For most traders the answer is defense, not offense: know the calendar, reduce or hedge exposure before top-tier releases, and expect stop slippage if you hold through them. Spreads can widen many times over for a few minutes.

EA users should check whether their systems include news filters. Mean-reversion and grid strategies are especially vulnerable to the one-directional bursts that follow big surprises.

Frequently Asked Questions

›What is the most important release for FX?

It rotates with the macro regime. In tightening/easing cycles, CPI and rate decisions dominate; in growth scares, employment and PMI data lead. US releases matter for nearly all pairs because the dollar is on one side of most trades.

›Should beginners trade the news?

Trading the release itself — no. Execution costs explode and moves whipsaw. Beginners benefit more from simply avoiding entries 30–60 minutes around top-tier events.

›Why did price move against a 'good' number?

Because expectations were higher, prior data was revised, or the market focused on a different component (e.g. wage growth inside a jobs report). The surprise, not the level, drives the move.
