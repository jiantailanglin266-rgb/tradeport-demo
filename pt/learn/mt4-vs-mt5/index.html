MT4 vs MT5: Which MetaTrader Platform Should You Use?

MetaTrader 4 and 5 look similar but differ in markets, order handling, backtesting and EA language. Here is a practical comparison to help you choose the right platform for your trading and automation.

Autor: M. RiveraPublicado: 28 de mai. de 2026Atualizado: 1 de jul. de 2026Dados verificados em: 1 de jul. de 2026

Pontos-Chave

  • ✓MT4 remains popular for FX-focused trading and its huge EA library.
  • ✓MT5 adds more timeframes, an economic calendar, depth of market and multi-asset support.
  • ✓MT5's strategy tester is far faster and supports multi-currency backtests.
  • ✓EAs are not compatible between platforms: MQL4 and MQL5 are different languages.
  • ✓Choose based on the EAs you plan to run and the markets you trade.

Sumário

  2. 2. Which platform is better for EA users?

What are the core differences?

MT4 was built for forex; MT5 is a multi-asset platform covering FX, stocks and futures with exchange-style features such as depth of market. MT5 offers 21 timeframes to MT4's 9, more built-in indicators, and a built-in economic calendar.

Order handling also differs: MT5 supports additional pending order types and, by default, position netting on exchange instruments, while both support hedging mode for FX at most brokers.

Which platform is better for EA users?

The deciding factor is usually the EA itself. MQL4 programs only run on MT4 and MQL5 programs only on MT5 — there is no compatibility layer. If the EA you want exists only for one platform, that settles it.

For developing and testing strategies, MT5 wins clearly: its strategy tester is multi-threaded, supports real tick data and multi-currency portfolios, and optimizes dramatically faster than MT4's single-threaded tester.

How should you actually choose?

If you trade FX only and rely on the mature MT4 EA ecosystem, MT4 remains a perfectly valid choice that brokers continue to support. If you are starting fresh, want faster backtesting, or trade CFDs on stocks and indices, start with MT5.

Many traders simply run both: MT4 for a legacy EA portfolio, MT5 for discretionary trading and research. Most brokers in our comparison offer both platforms on the same account base.

Perguntas Frequentes

›Is MT4 being discontinued?

MetaQuotes has focused development on MT5 and restricted new MT4 server licenses, but brokers continue to operate existing MT4 servers due to trader demand. The practical risk is gradual, not immediate.

›Can I convert an MQL4 EA to MQL5?

Not automatically. The languages and execution models differ enough that conversion is a rewrite with testing, not a file migration.

›Do MT4 and MT5 accounts share login credentials?

No. They are separate server platforms; brokers issue separate accounts (or separate logins) for each.
