Leverage and Margin Explained: How Small Deposits Control Big Positions

Leverage lets you control a large position with a small deposit called margin. This guide explains margin requirements, margin calls and stop-outs — and why effective leverage, not maximum leverage, is what actually matters.

Autor: K. TanakaRevisado por: H. SuzukiPublicado: 16 de mai. de 2026Atualizado: 22 de jul. de 2026Dados verificados em: 22 de jul. de 2026

How does leverage actually work?

Leverage is borrowed exposure. With 1:100 leverage, a $1,000 margin deposit controls a $100,000 position. Every price movement is calculated on the full $100,000 — so a 1% move equals your entire margin.

This cuts both ways. Leverage does not change the market's behavior; it changes how much of your account each pip is worth. That is why two traders with the same strategy can have completely different outcomes.

What are margin calls and stop-outs?

Required margin is the collateral reserved per position. Your margin level (equity ÷ used margin) is the key gauge. When losses push it below the broker's margin call level, you are warned to add funds or reduce positions.

If it falls further to the stop-out level, the broker automatically closes positions, starting with the largest loser, to protect remaining equity. Know both thresholds before you trade — they vary by broker and account type.

Why does effective leverage matter more than maximum?

Maximum leverage is what the broker allows; effective leverage is what you actually use — total position value divided by equity. A trader with 1:1000 available who opens small positions may run effective leverage of 3:1, which is conservative.

Control effective leverage through position sizing: decide the percent of equity you risk per trade (commonly 1–2%), derive the position size from your stop distance, and the leverage takes care of itself.

Perguntas Frequentes

›Is higher maximum leverage always more dangerous?

No. Danger comes from effective leverage — how large your positions are relative to equity. High maximum leverage with small positions can be safer than low leverage used at its limit.

›What happens at stop-out?

The broker force-closes your open positions, typically the largest losing one first, until margin level recovers above the stop-out threshold. It exists to prevent your balance from going deeply negative.

›What leverage should a beginner use?

Focus on risking no more than 1–2% of equity per trade with a defined stop. That rule automatically keeps effective leverage low regardless of what the account offers.
