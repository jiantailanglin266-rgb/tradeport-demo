What Is Forex Trading? A Beginner's Guide to the Currency Market

Forex is the global market where currencies are exchanged. This guide explains how currency pairs work, when the market is open, who participates, and what you need to know before placing your first trade.

Autor: K. TanakaRevisado por: H. SuzukiPublicado: 2 may 2026Actualizado: 18 jul 2026Datos verificados: 18 jul 2026

Puntos Clave

  • ✓Forex is the largest financial market in the world, trading 24 hours a day, five days a week.
  • ✓Currencies are always traded in pairs: buying EUR/USD means buying euros and selling dollars.
  • ✓Prices are quoted in pips — the smallest standard unit of price movement.
  • ✓Most retail trading uses leverage, which magnifies both profits and losses.
  • ✓Start with a demo account and a written risk plan before using real money.

What is the forex market?

The foreign exchange (forex, or FX) market is where the world's currencies are bought and sold. Banks, companies, governments and individual traders exchange one currency for another — for trade, investment, or speculation — making it the largest and most liquid financial market in the world.

Unlike a stock exchange, forex has no central location. Trading happens electronically between participants around the globe, which is why the market runs 24 hours a day from Monday morning in Asia to Friday evening in New York.

How do currency pairs work?

Currencies are always quoted in pairs, such as EUR/USD or USD/JPY. The first currency is the base, the second is the quote. If EUR/USD is 1.0850, one euro costs 1.0850 US dollars. Buying the pair means you expect the base currency to strengthen against the quote currency.

Price changes are measured in pips. For most pairs a pip is 0.0001; for yen pairs it is 0.01. Understanding pip value is essential because it links price movement to actual profit or loss in your account currency.

How should a beginner start?

Start with education, not deposits. Learn how leverage, margin and stop-loss orders work, then practice on a demo account until your process feels routine. Only then consider a small live account with money you can afford to lose.

Choose a regulated broker, keep position sizes small, and record every trade in a journal. Consistency and risk control matter far more than finding a perfect entry signal.

Preguntas Frecuentes

›How much money do I need to start trading forex?

Many brokers accept small deposits, but the practical minimum is whatever lets you risk 1–2% per trade with proper stop losses. Start on a demo account first — it costs nothing.

›Is forex trading gambling?

It can be if you trade without a plan. With defined risk per trade, a tested strategy and a journal, it becomes structured decision-making under uncertainty — still risky, but not random.

›When is the best time to trade?

Liquidity peaks when the London and New York sessions overlap (roughly 12:00–16:00 UTC). Beginners should avoid major news releases until they understand volatility spikes.
